# N08: evidence-and-mechanisms

Cards read from [evidence-and-mechanisms.md](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md) — 150 in all. Part of the [claim inventory](../claim-inventory.md).

- **N08:1** (argument, A, field) The loss of a developer's conceptual model under agentic delegation is real in the specific sense the evidence supports, and narrower than the popular framing. From: [Finding](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#finding).
- **N08:2** (argument, A, field) Naur's argument that a program's theory lives in its programmers is a philosophical position, but it predicts exactly what four decades of program-comprehension research observed. Source: Naur 1985. From: [Finding](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#finding).
- **N08:3** (argument, A, person, person) The primary product of programming is not the program text but a theory held by the programmers, in Ryle's sense of an intellectual capacity to explain, justify, and extend. Source: Naur 1985. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:4** (argument, A, person, person) The theory-holder knows three things documented products cannot carry: how each part maps to the affairs of the world, why each part is what it is, and how to respond constructively to a demand for modification. Source: Naur 1985. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:5** (argument, A, person, person) Responding to a modification demand depends on perceiving similarity between the new demand and existing facilities, a similarity between aspects of the world that cannot be reduced to any limited set of criteria or rules. Where: on a demand for modification. Source: Naur 1985. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:6** (definition, A, team, team) A program dies when the programmer team possessing its theory is dissolved; death becomes visible when demands for modifications cannot be intelligently answered. Source: Naur 1985, pp. 253–261. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:7** (argument, A, repository, person) Program revival, that is reestablishing the theory of a program merely from the documentation, is strictly impossible. Source: Naur 1985, pp. 253–261. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:8** (practice, A, person, person) Naur's remedy for onboarding is working in close contact with the programmers who already possess the theory, doing the relevant things under supervision, because this is knowledge-how rather than knowledge-that. Where: onboarding. Source: Naur 1985. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:9** (argument, A, change, agent) For agent-written code, the agent's session held whatever mapping and justification produced the code, and that session ends; if Naur is right the code is born in the dead state unless a person builds the theory alongside it. Where: when the agent's session ends. Source: Naur 1985. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:10** (argument, A, repository, person) Rebuilding the theory of agent-written code later from the text is the costly revival Naur advises against; this is an argument, not a measurement. Source: Naur 1985. From: [Naur: the theory is not in the text](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#naur-the-theory-is-not-in-the-text).
- **N08:11** (mechanism, A, person, person) Brooks proposed comprehension as top-down hypothesis refinement across a chain of domains from problem to program, with beacons in the code confirming or refuting hypotheses. Source: Brooks 1983. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:12** (finding, -, person, person) Experts hold programming plans (stereotyped code fragments for goals) and rules of programming discourse; when code violates those conventions, expert advantage over novices largely disappears. Source: Soloway and Ehrlich 1984. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:13** (finding, -, person, person) Wiedenbeck confirmed beacons empirically. Source: Wiedenbeck 1986. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:14** (finding, Q, person, person) Programmers combine top-down and bottom-up strategies opportunistically, asking why, how, and what questions, and forming conjectures whose confirmation depends on the availability of rationale. Source: Letovsky 1987. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:15** (definition, -, person, person) Pennington distinguished the program model (control flow, built first) from the domain or situation model (what the program does in world terms, data flow, function). Source: Pennington 1987. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:16** (finding, -, person, person) Programmers who built the situation model, not just the program model, performed better on modification tasks. Where: on modification tasks. Source: Pennington 1987. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:17** (finding, -, person, person) The Integrated Metamodel combines top-down (domain), program, and situation models plus a knowledge base, with maintainers switching among them; large-scale maintenance was dominated by understanding time. Where: during software maintenance and evolution. Source: von Mayrhauser and Vans 1995. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:18** (finding, A, field, tool) Tools mostly support the program model and rarely the situation or domain model. Source: Storey 2005/2006. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:19** (finding, Q, team, person) From two surveys and eleven interviews at Microsoft, much of what developers need is implicit knowledge held only in colleagues' memories; the hardest questions were about design rationale and the intent behind code, recovered by exploring code and interrupting teammates. Figures: two surveys and eleven interviews at Microsoft. Source: LaToza, Venolia, and DeLine 2006. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:20** (finding, -, team, person) Questions about why code was implemented a particular way were among the information needs most often left unanswered. Source: Ko, DeLine, and Venolia 2007. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:21** (finding, M, module, person) Authorship-based ownership metrics were associated with lower defect density in Windows components. Source: Bird et al. 2011. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:22** (argument, A, repository, agent) Wheeler, a position paper with no empirical data, argues that AI-generated code severs the inference from authorship to comprehension that metrics such as truck factor and degree-of-authorship rely on. Source: Wheeler 2026. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:23** (argument, A, repository) Wheeler states a falsifiable prediction that systems with high authorship-derived truck factor but low comprehension will show incident-resolution failures the metric cannot predict. Source: Wheeler 2026. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:24** (definition, A) Green and Petre's cognitive dimensions framework names properties of a notation such as hidden dependencies, premature commitment, and role expressiveness; it is a vocabulary for what makes structure hard to see, not a measurement of comprehension. Source: Green and Petre 1996. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:25** (definition, A, person, person) The model is layered: the program layer (control and data flow) is recoverable from text with effort; the situation layer (world mapping, function) is expensive and what modification depends on; the rationale (why) is largely undocumented and socially held. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:26** (argument, A, team, person) The rationale layer is what Naur said cannot be rebuilt from text and what LaToza and Ko found developers already struggled to recover before AI. Source: Naur 1985; LaToza 2006; Ko 2007. From: [Program comprehension: what a mental model of code consists of](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#program-comprehension-what-a-mental-model-of-code-consists-of).
- **N08:27** (measurement, C, person, person) Kosmyna et al. measured EEG connectivity (dDTF), NLP features of essays, human and AI scoring, and post-session interviews in 54 participants across LLM, Search Engine, and Brain-only arms over three sessions, 18 of whom returned for a crossover session 4. Figures: 54 participants, three arms, three sessions, 18 returned for session 4. Source: Kosmyna et al. 2025. From: [AI-specific evidence, 2023–2026](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#ai-specific-evidence-20232026).
- **N08:28** (finding, C, person, person) Brain-only showed the strongest and most distributed connectivity, LLM the weakest. Source: Kosmyna et al. 2025. From: [AI-specific evidence, 2023–2026](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#ai-specific-evidence-20232026).
- **N08:29** (measurement, C, person, person) In session 1, 83.3% (15/18) of the LLM group failed to produce a correct quotation from the essay they had just written versus 11.1% (2/18) in each other group (p < .001); by session 2 most participants in all groups could quote. Figures: 83.3% (15/18) LLM vs 11.1% (2/18) other groups, p < .001, session 1. Where: after the essay they had just written. Source: Kosmyna et al. 2025. From: [AI-specific evidence, 2023–2026](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#ai-specific-evidence-20232026).
- **N08:30** (definition, -, person, person) Cognitive debt is a condition in which repeated reliance on external systems like LLMs replaces the effortful cognitive processes required for independent thinking. Source: Kosmyna et al. 2025. From: [AI-specific evidence, 2023–2026](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#ai-specific-evidence-20232026).
- **N08:31** (argument, -, field) Kosmyna's stated limitations: participants from one geographic area and a few institutions, not gender balanced; ChatGPT only; EEG connectivity only with poor spatial resolution; findings context-dependent on essay writing in an educational setting and may not generalize across tasks. Source: Kosmyna et al. 2025. From: [AI-specific evidence, 2023–2026](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#ai-specific-evidence-20232026).

- **N08:33** (argument, A, field) Kosmyna et al. coined the term cognitive debt, measured essay writing, and is not evidence about code. Source: Kosmyna et al. 2025. From: [AI-specific evidence, 2023–2026](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#ai-specific-evidence-20232026).
- **N08:34** (measurement, C, person, person) METR's RCT of 16 developers on 246 real issues in mature repositories (about 10 years old, >1.1M lines, developers averaging 5 years and 1,500 commits), mostly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, found AI-allowed issues took 19% longer (95% CI roughly +2% to +39%). Figures: 24% forecast speedup, 20% believed speedup. Source: METR 2025. From: [AI-specific evidence, 2023–2026](../evidence-and-mechanisms.md#ai-specific-evidence-20232026).
